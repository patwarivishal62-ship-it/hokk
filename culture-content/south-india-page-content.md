## 1. PAGE POSITIONING STATEMENT

The South's page is built on one metaphor: structure. Southern textiles engineer their beauty — borders joined to bodies so the cloth tears first, patterns dyed into yarn before weaving, motifs floated by hand into transparent ground. The page positions the sari as inheritance ("stored like bullion, lent like jewellery") and the temple town as the loom's patron. Tone: precise, confident, slightly technical — the reader should finish able to explain korvai and ikat to someone else.

## 4. THE FIVE TRADITIONS (shipped)

1. **Kanjivaram — Kanchipuram.** Engineering: korvai interlock, petni pallu join, three cloths in one sari; Kancheepuram Silk GI (2005); Devanga/Saligar weaving communities; temple-border vocabulary.
2. **Pochampally Ikat — Telangana.** Pattern before cloth: tying, dyeing, aligning; GI registered mid-2000s (sources differ 2004/2005 — stated honestly); thousands of weaving families.
3. **Kasavu — Kerala.** Restraint as technique: kora cotton, one gold band; Balaramapuram GI-registered; mundu/veshti/set mundu grammar; Onam as kasavu's festival.
4. **Uppada Jamdani — Andhra.** Hand-timing as pattern: kandi stick, discontinuous wefts, no jacquard; GI 2009; Bengal technique carried to the Coromandel.
5. **Mysore Silk — Karnataka.** Certification as craft protection: one certified supply chain, GI 2005, coded saris; crepe drape; inbuti pallu dots.
