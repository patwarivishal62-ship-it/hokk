## 1. PAGE POSITIONING STATEMENT

The North East page argues one idea: in this region, cloth is identity. The valley's two wild silks (muga, eri) are found nowhere else; the hills' backstrap looms produce textiles that declare community, achievement and belonging. The page must feel distinct from every other region — less courtly than the North, less architectural than the South — organised around the loom strapped to the weaver's own body and the silks raised beside the house. Educates without exoticising; the region is presented as a living, contemporary craft economy, not a remote museum.

## 4. THE FIVE TRADITIONS (chapter structure, shipped in template)

Each tradition chapter carries: eyebrow (place), H2 title, technique + material tags, short description, full body, one distinctive characteristic, related-traditions line, CTA. Bodies cover: what it is, where, who makes it, materials, process, motifs, colour, contemporary use, authenticity notes — all preloaded and editable as blocks.

1. **Muga Silk — Assam.** Wild golden silk; GI 2007 (Assam's first); reared on som/soalu; woven in Sualkuchi into mekhela chadors; lustre deepens with age. No superlatives beyond the documented fibre facts.
2. **Eri Silk — Assam.** The peace silk — open cocoons, moth emerges; spun not reeled; matte, warm, dye-loving; today a global slow-fashion fibre. No GI claim made (correctly — none exists).
3. **Naga Shawls — Nagaland.** Backstrap-loom identity cloth; patterns historically tied to achievement (headhunting cloths, feasts of merit) stated as documented history; Chakhesang shawl's GI registration noted.
4. **Shaphee Lanphee & Moirang Phee — Manipur.** Both GI-registered; shaphee lanphee's warrior-bestowal account flagged as historical account; moirang phee's border and name documented; Manipur's loin-loom culture.
5. **Puan — Mizoram.** Mizo woven wardrobe; puanchei ceremonial; women's household production; stripe grammar known by name.
