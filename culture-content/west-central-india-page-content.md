## 1. PAGE POSITIONING STATEMENT

The West & Central page is the technique summit: every major way of patterning cloth lives here — double ikat (dye the threads), bandhani (tie the cloth), Chanderi/Maheshwari (weave the border), Ajrakh (print the resist). The organising idea is "pattern as puzzle, solved before the cloth exists." The page also tells the region's institutional story — Kutch's craft renaissance, Rehwa's Maheshwar model, weaver-owned companies — positioning the West as where India's craft-revival playbook was written.

## 4. THE FIVE TRADITIONS (shipped)

1. **Patan Patola — Gujarat.** The summit: both thread systems dyed and aligned; Salvi families' centuries; twelfth-century migration account labelled as tradition; GI 2013; reversible proof; year-per-sari labour honestly framed.
2. **Bandhani — Kutch & Jamnagar.** Knot as unit: Khatri community, fingernail tying, ring fields; gharcholu wedding grammar; Jamnagari Bandhani GI 2010 and Kutch Bandhani GI 2021; the dimple tell.
3. **Chanderi — MP.** Transparency as identity: silk warp, handspun-fine cotton weft, zari borders; Ansari weaving community; GI 2005 (among India's earliest).
4. **Maheshwari — MP.** Border-first design; Ahilyabai Holkar's eighteenth-century court origin (documented account); named motifs (hans, panchkui); Rehwa Society revival; GI 2012.
5. **Ajrakh — Kutch.** Process as print: up to sixteen stages, indigo + madder + resist; Khatri printers of Ajrakhpur/Dhamadka; name etymology given as candidates, not fact; no GI claim made (none verified) — craft standing carried by process and community.
